00Beginner Start

Prepare to think like an Enterprise Architect — even if you're starting from zero.

This guide turns the P_SAPEA preparation journey into a sequence: understand the idea → recognise it in a messy business situation → make a decision → defend it. Core rule: the unit of study is the decision, not the isolated fact.

📖 Learn

Understand the vocabulary, architecture layers and SAP methods.

🔧 Apply

Use the ideas on a connected enterprise scenario.

🛡️ Defend

Explain why your recommendation is appropriate, including trade-offs and risks.

How to use this workspace

  • Read the beginner sections in order using the left-hand map.
  • Do the connected case without looking at the model answer.
  • Score yourself using the rubric.
  • Repeat until you can explain the reasoning clearly and efficiently.
  • Use Practice to check domain knowledge as you go, and the Study Desk below to keep notes per sheet.

01Current Exam Model

What changed?

Since 2026, SAP has been moving its certification portfolio from multiple-choice recognition tests toward performance-based practical assessments. SAP describes two broad practical formats: System-Based Assessment (execute and validate tasks in a live system) and Scenario-Based Assessment (reason through a connected business situation). P_SAPEA uses the Scenario-Based Assessment format — typically completed either through an AI-guided roleplay interview or by recording a video for human review.

Important

Do not hard-code exam mechanics. Question counts, timing, and pass marks for the legacy multiple-choice version of this exam (40 questions, 3 hours, 80% pass mark, Wanderlust case study) do not reliably describe the current Scenario-Based Assessment. The exact structure, duration, attempt limits and scoring method are shown on the official P_SAPEA certification page and the Scenario-Based Assessment FAQ. Check both immediately before your exam.

02Decision Method

KNOW → RECOGNIZE → DECIDE → DEFEND

Use the same mental loop for every topic.

❓ What is it?

Learn the concept, artifact, method or architecture layer.

🔍 When does it matter?

Spot the signal in a scenario: duplication, risk, strategic change, integration complexity, regulatory constraint, and so on.

⚡ What would you do?

Select an approach that fits the business objective, constraints, maturity and target state.

💡 Why this choice?

Explain rationale, alternatives, trade-offs, risks, sequencing and validation.

Example

"Given [objective] and [constraints], I recommend [decision] because [reason]. The main trade-off is [trade-off]. I would manage the risk by [mitigation] and validate it through [evidence]."

03Skill Area 1

Enterprise Architecture Framework & Toolset

Think of the framework as a way to move from business intent to a coherent, governable architecture and roadmap.

Vision → Requirements & Decisions → Business → Application / Data / Technology → Opportunities & Solutions → Roadmap
  • Business strategy must translate into explicit architecture requirements.
  • Architecture artifacts are communication and decision tools, not decoration.
  • SAP reference architecture content helps connect business needs to SAP solution possibilities.
  • SAP LeanIX and SAP Signavio can support different parts of the architecture and process picture.
  • Always ask: who needs this artifact, what decision does it support, and what evidence feeds it?

04Skill Area 2

Architecture Vision & Roadmap

The architect's job is not simply to draw a target state. The job is to create a credible path from the current situation to a better business outcome.

Vision

  • Business objectives
  • Stakeholders
  • Current-state pain
  • Target capabilities
  • Scope and constraints
  • Success measures

Roadmap

  • Prioritised initiatives
  • Dependencies
  • Sequencing
  • Value and risk
  • Transition states
  • Governance and decision points
Exam Tip

When the scenario gives you many problems, do not solve them all at once. Establish the strategic objective, identify dependencies, then sequence the work.

05Skill Area 3

Business Architecture

Start with what the business needs to be able to do before jumping to software.

Definition

Capability

What the business is able to do, independent of the detailed process used to do it.

⚠️ Trap: confusing a capability with a process or application.

Definition

Process

How work flows from one activity to another to create an outcome.

⚠️ Trap: optimising a process without checking whether it supports the strategic objective.

When reading a case, ask:

  • What business outcome is changing?
  • Which capabilities must improve or appear?
  • Which processes are creating the problem?
  • Which stakeholders own the outcome?
  • What evidence would prove the architecture is working?

06Skill Area 4

Data, Application & Technology Architecture

📊 What information?

Ownership, quality, lifecycle, privacy, residency, integration and access.

⚙️ What capabilities are supported?

Application responsibilities, boundaries, interfaces, duplication and fit with business capabilities.

🖥️ What enables it?

Platforms, infrastructure, security, connectivity, operations and non-functional requirements.

Definition

Clean Core

Use the core product as intended and avoid unnecessary modifications that make upgrades and lifecycle management harder. Where appropriate, extensions can be designed outside or alongside the core using SAP's supported extensibility approaches.

Definition

Public vs private cloud

Do not memorise "public is good" or "private is good." Evaluate standardisation, fit-to-standard expectations, required flexibility, operational model, integration, lifecycle and business constraints.

Integration & regulatory thinking

  • Identify the business event or data exchange first.
  • Then decide the integration responsibility and pattern.
  • Check security, privacy, residency and regulatory constraints.
  • Prefer the simplest sustainable architecture that meets the requirement.

07Connected Case

The Wanderlust-style case

Use this fictional case to practise the same reasoning pattern without relying on memorised questions. It is inspired by SAP's classic case-study format, not a reproduction of any current exam content.

Scenario

A global manufacturer wants to enter a fast-growing electric-vehicle market. Its business units operate independently, several ERP systems have evolved separately, customer data is duplicated, and leadership wants faster product launches without losing local regulatory control.

The CIO asks for an architecture recommendation and a realistic transformation roadmap.

Six linked decisions

  • Objective: What is the primary business outcome?
  • Constraints: Which business, technology, regulatory and organisational constraints matter most?
  • Business: Which capabilities and processes must change?
  • Architecture: What target-state principles and boundaries would you propose?
  • Roadmap: What should happen first, and why?
  • Governance: What decisions, stakeholders and measures keep the transformation on track?
Important

📌 Do the case before reading examples. Your first objective is not to find a perfect architecture. It is to demonstrate coherent reasoning.

08Defense Pattern

The 9-step architecture defense

1. Context

"The situation I understand is…"

2. Objective

"The outcome we need is…"

3. Constraints

"The constraints that shape the decision are…"

4. Recommendation

"I would recommend…"

5. Architecture rationale

"This fits because…"

6. Trade-off

"The main trade-off is…"

7. Risk

"The largest risk is…"

8. Roadmap

"I would sequence the work as…"

9. Validation

"I would validate the decision through…"

Tip

If you get stuck, return to: business objective → constraints → decision → rationale → trade-off → next step.

Since the current exam is a spoken/recorded roleplay, practise saying this pattern out loud against the clock — not just writing it down.
